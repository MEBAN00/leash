## Design Context

### Users
- **Everyday consumers** handing an AI shopping agent permission to spend from their PayPal wallet. They are cautious: the question in their head is "what if it goes wrong?"
- **Hackathon judges** (PayPal AI Hackathon) watching a ~3 minute demo video and clicking through a live build. They judge technical depth, design, impact, innovation and presentation.
- Jobs: set spending rules in plain English, watch an agent shop, approve or decline flagged purchases from a phone, and audit every decision afterwards (and refund).
- Contexts: laptop for setup and watching runs; phone for approvals, often a quick glance-and-tap.

### Brand Personality
- **Calm, trustworthy, precise.**
- Quiet confidence, like a well-made financial tool. Nothing flashy, nothing alarming.
- The rules and the decisions are the hero, not the AI. Copy is plain, short and specific ("$60.00 is over your $40.00 ask-me limit"), never hype.
- Emotional goal: the user feels *in control*, even while something else spends for them.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Light theme only.** Warm, paper-like neutrals tinted toward the brand hue; never pure white or black.
- Brand hue: a deep ink / petrol (blue-green). Deliberately not PayPal blue and not the purple/cyan "AI" palette.
- Decision colors carry meaning everywhere: **ALLOW** = green, **ASK** = amber, **DENY** = vermilion. Used as ink, not as glowing fills.
- Signature idea: every policy decision is a **receipt**: a slip with the cart, a stamp (ALLOW / ASK / DENY) and the reasons. The audit trail is a stack of receipts.
- Craft references: Apple's design approach (restraint, typographic hierarchy, physical, interruptible motion, materials used with purpose) and the frontend-design skill's anti-slop rules.
- The user likes the look of a fintech/payment provider called "BACHS" (no public site found yet; revisit if a link is provided).
- Anti-references: dark "AI dashboard" with neon glows, gradient text, glassmorphism everywhere, identical card grids, hero-metric templates, PayPal clone.

### Design Principles
1. **Decisions are legible at a glance.** Every ALLOW / ASK / DENY shows its reason in one plain sentence. Never make the user dig for "why".
2. **Calm by default, loud only when it matters.** Neutral surfaces; color appears where money moves or a decision is needed.
3. **The human is always in control.** Approval and refund are one tap; nothing irreversible happens without a visible, explainable rule.
4. **Physical, purposeful motion.** Springs and ease-out curves that convey state change (a receipt printing, a stamp landing); always respect reduced motion.
5. **Phone-first for approvals, desk-first for setup.** Adapt layouts to context rather than shrinking them.

### Accessibility
- WCAG 2.2 AA contrast; decision states never rely on color alone (stamp text + icon).
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`; full keyboard support; visible focus.
